# 133 - The plan of 2026-10-15: tiny experts, the public gate, and tasks a small model can build

Written by the session on 2026-10-15 from the human's words of that night, four recorded seats
(`research/133-seat-*.md`), and two inventories of what the repository already admits
(`scratchpad admissions-notes.md`, folded into section 3 below). Draft for a panel; nothing here is
decided. The human's words, kept because the plan answers them:

> MoE models that activate the fewest parameters possible, to train very specific parts. A way to
> look inside the model: which experts do math, which code. Instead of adding noise, reinforce
> specific experts with data distilled from frontier models, producing tiny local models for very
> specific cases, connected through Hydra so everyone has real AI: minimal open weights on CPU, a
> PC from 2010 onward. Also: SECURITY.md reads as full of holes or out of date; resolve all before
> going public. Tasks must be very specific, with an objective, the problem, the cases, the test
> first, implementation-independent, typed like TypeScript or Pydantic, with an expected latency,
> so that small agents can build them in small changes. Review the plan with panels.

## 2. The idea, read against the seats

The MiMo seat (haiku, run `01a14295-9d3f`, memo `133-seat-haiku-mimo.md`) read the model cards,
the report and the dataset. Verified there: MiMo-V2.6 Pro is a 1.02T total, 42B active MoE with
384 routed experts and 8 active, Flash is 309B total and 15B active, both MIT by their cards, with
a 5-layer multi-token drafter. The RL stage samples 1,568 prompts by 16 rollouts per step on
thousands of GPUs, 2.6M USD for Pro and 0.9M for Flash. From the report's summary, unverified in
the PDF: a dynamic sampler drops task groups where every rollout passes or fails, binary test
rewards for code, a hack agent run until it finds no exploit, and the 9B model is plain SFT of
Qwen3.5-9B on 77.4B tokens, not RL. What is open: weights; a verl fork with reproduction code for
five RL environments; a dataset of 7,780 tasks in five subsets whose rows name a Docker image per
task, all linux/amd64, up to 6 GB each. On this Mac the parquet reads fine, one task means pulling
an amd64 image under emulation, and an RL step is out of reach. The codex seat before it
(`133-seat-codex-mimo.md`) had no web tool under the gate and holds the transcript's claims only.

What this gives the idea, by the seat's reading and the session's: the task shape, a prompt plus a
rule reward plus a per-task verifier, is the split `bank.rs` and `score.rs` already make, and the
dataset's schema is a template for a bank record; group selection needs `attempt.rs` to draw
several answers per task where it draws one at temperature zero today; and the leak check, no
commit after the base counts, is cheap to add to the bank build. One rented GPU for hours buys a
LoRA SFT of a 1B to 4B model on the export and the pairs file, by inference, not measured. The
pairs nobody trains on are still the only training signal Hydra writes.

The MoE seat (gemini, `01a14291-ced3`, memo `133-seat-gemini-moe.md`) gives the model table and
the legal quotes. Its numbers are mostly marked estimated, and several of its citations point at a
repository or site root rather than a page, so the session takes the shape and not the digits.
The shape: the open MoE models with under 1B active parameters it found are Meta's MobileMoE
family, 0.27B to 0.92B active, under a non-commercial licence, which fails the human's "everyone
has real AI"; the smallest Apache-2.0 MoE is OLMoE-1B-7B, 1.3B active but 6.9B total and 4.2 GB
at Q4, which does not fit a 4 GB machine. Total parameters, not active ones, set the RAM and the
cache behaviour, so on a 2010 desktop with SSE4.2, no AVX, dual-channel DDR3 and 4 to 8 GB, a
dense model under 1B at Q4 is the practical path: Qwen2.5-0.5B, Apache-2.0, 0.35 GB, an
estimated 10 to 18 tokens per second there; a 7B-total MoE drops to 1 to 2. Looking inside: on CPU,
Transformers' `output_router_logits` returns per-layer router logits for any MoE it loads, and
NNsight traces the gates; llama.cpp has no flag for it and needs instrumentation. Published
analyses disagree on specialisation: OLMoE's 64 fine-grained experts cluster by domain, Mixtral's
8 coarse experts cluster by token syntax and position, so "which expert does math" is a property of
the model, not of MoE. Training only some experts: freezing the router and fine-tuning chosen
experts is published (DeepSeekMoE), as are expert pruning (MoE-Pruner, HOPE) and distilling chosen
experts into a dense network; on this Mac a LoRA fits models under about 1.5B total, a 7B MoE
does not; one rented GPU for hours does OLMoE.

The legal part, quoted by the seat on 2026-10-15 from the three vendors' pages: Anthropic's
consumer and commercial terms forbid using outputs "to develop or train any artificial intelligence
or machine learning algorithms or models" that compete; OpenAI's terms forbid using output "to
develop models that compete with OpenAI"; Google's Gemini API terms forbid using the Services "to
develop models that compete with the Services". Using frontier outputs as evaluation labels or as
a judge does not update any parameters and is a separate activity, which the apprentice already
does through the panel's grading. Distillation into a model, the human's "sacando de los frontier
models", is the forbidden activity under all three, and the plan does not include it. The session
spot-checked that the seat's main cited pages exist (section 7).

## 4. The task format: a contract a small model can build against

The human asked for tasks that are typed, test first, independent of language and implementation,
with an expected latency, so a small model builds each in a few lines. The repository already does
half of this in the grader's differential tests. The proposal makes it a named thing.

A contract is a directory `contracts/<name>/` with three files:

- `contract.json`: JSON Schema for `input` and `output`, plus `latency` as `{ "p50_ms": N,
  "p95_ms": M, "on": "this Mac, M2 8 GB" }` and `errors` as a list of named refusals the
  implementation may return instead of an output.
- `cases.jsonl`: one line per case, `{ "name", "input", "expect" }`, where `expect` is either an
  `output` or an `error` name. Written before any implementation. A case may carry `"skip_on":
  ["linux"]`.
- `README.md`: the objective in one paragraph, the problem it solves in one, and which Hydra
  surface consumes the output.

An implementation is any executable that reads one `input` as JSON on stdin and writes one
`output` or `{ "error": name }` as JSON on stdout. The runner, `hydra-puppets contract run
<name> -- <command...>`, feeds every case, compares with the house JSON equality (`hydra_pyjson`),
measures wall time per case, and prints pass, fail and the latency percentiles against the
contract. The runner is one Rust file and its own tests feed it a stub implementation. The
schema is validated with the `jsonschema` crate, pinned, or by a hand-written checker if the
panel prefers no new dependency.

A build ticket that carries a contract names it under `## Contract` and lists its cases under
`## Cases`; `## Do` is then "make the runner green". Size is declared: `Size: haiku` for a ticket a
`claude-haiku-5-5` run builds in one pass, `Size: opus` otherwise. The alias `haiku` resolves to
Haiku 5.5 on Claude Code 2.1.301, measured in the records of this night's two haiku seats.

1. Order: 339, 348 and 338 first, because 339 and 338 are the two things found tonight that make a
   record lie by omission and 348 is a relative path the gate lets through, and each is one afternoon. Then 134 with the human, then 340 and 341 the
   same day. Publication after 340 lands and `security` is green on master.

   If no MoE in the 1B-active class runs on this Mac, the idea's first version is a dense model
   under 1B plus a classifier head, which is map 109's answer already.
3. Distillation from frontier outputs into a model: do not plan it until the gemini seat's quotes
   of the three vendors' terms are read; using frontier outputs as evaluation labels is a
   separate question and the apprentice does it today through the panel's grading.
4. Comparing Hydra with mature projects: measure on the thing Hydra alone does, a gated,
   recorded, multi-vendor run with a replayable gate, and say so; a breadth comparison loses
   on purpose.
5. The 2010 PC: hold it as a constraint on the serving side only, SSE4.2, no AVX2, 4 GB; the
   model that satisfies it is under 1B at Q4 and the gemini seat says what that buys.
